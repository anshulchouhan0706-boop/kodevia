How long does it take to build a website or app?

What sets the timeline of a website or mobile app project, the stages it moves through, and how to keep yours on schedule.

"When can it go live?" is usually the second question after "how much?". The honest answer depends on scope, but the timeline is far more predictable once you know what each stage involves and what slows projects down.

The stages every project goes through

  1. Discover. Understanding the business, the users and the must-have features. This sets the scope everything else depends on.
  2. Design. Flows and wireframes first, then a clickable prototype, then the final visual design.
  3. Build. Development in short rounds, with a staging link you can open and test as it grows.
  4. Test and launch. Checking every screen on real devices, fixing issues, going live, and for apps, store review.

What makes a project shorter

  • A clear goal and a short must-have list for the first version
  • Content, photos and brand files ready at the start
  • One person who gives feedback and approves quickly
  • Using proven building blocks instead of inventing everything from scratch

What makes it longer

  • New features added halfway through
  • Waiting on content, logins or approvals
  • Integrations with outside systems, such as payments, ERPs or delivery partners
  • Building for many platforms at once
  • App store reviews, which you do not control

Websites and apps are different

A marketing website with a handful of pages is usually the quickest kind of project. An online store, a booking system or a customer portal takes longer because every feature needs logic and testing. A mobile app adds device testing, store listings and review time on top of the build.

Launch small, then grow

The fastest route to a live product is a focused first version: the features that deliver your goal, done well. Everything on the nice-to-have list can follow in later rounds, guided by what real customers actually use.
