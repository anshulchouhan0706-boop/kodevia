Website or mobile app: which does your business need first?

A plain guide to choosing between a website and a mobile app as your first digital product, with the questions that decide it.

It is one of the first questions we hear: should we build a website or an app? The honest answer is that most businesses need a website first, and some businesses need an app as well. Here is how to tell which group you are in.

Start with how people find you

People discover businesses through search, links and recommendations. All of those lead to a web page, not an app store. A website is the front door that search engines can read, that you can share in a message, and that opens instantly on any phone or laptop without an install.

An app, by contrast, has to be found in an app store, downloaded and opened. That is a big ask for someone who has never heard of you. Apps are rarely how a customer meets a business for the first time.

When a website is enough

  • Your customers visit occasionally: to learn about you, compare options or make an enquiry.
  • You sell products online and want the checkout to work for everyone, including first-time visitors.
  • You need to rank in search results for what you offer.
  • You want to change content often without waiting for app store reviews.

A well-built website can also feel very close to an app on a phone: fast, smooth and usable with one thumb.

When an app earns its place

  • Customers use your service often, such as ordering, booking or tracking every week.
  • You need features a browser handles poorly, such as reliable notifications, offline use, the camera or location in the background.
  • Your customers are already loyal and would benefit from a faster, saved, signed-in experience.

Food ordering, pet care, retail loyalty and booking services are common examples. In our own work the apps we build usually sit next to a website, not instead of one.

Questions that decide it

  1. How often will one person use it? Rarely points to a website. Weekly or daily can justify an app.
  2. How will new customers find you? If the answer is search or social media, you need a website either way.
  3. Does it need anything only an app can do? If not, start on the web.
  4. Who will keep it running? An app means two store listings and regular updates for new phone versions.

A sensible order

For most businesses the path is: launch a fast, clear website; learn what customers actually do on it; then build an app for the repeat customers once you know which features they would open every day. Designing both from one plan, with one team, keeps the brand and the experience consistent across the two.
