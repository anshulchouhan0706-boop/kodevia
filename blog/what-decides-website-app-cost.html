What decides the cost of a website or app

The factors that actually move the price of a website or mobile app project, so you can plan a budget and compare quotes fairly.

Two quotes for "a website" can be very different, and both can be fair. The difference is almost always in scope. Knowing what drives the cost helps you plan a budget and compare quotes on equal terms.

1. The number of unique screens

A page that repeats a layout, such as a list of blog posts, costs far less than a screen that needs its own design and logic. Count the distinct screens, not the total pages: a home page, a product page, a checkout and an account area are four very different pieces of work.

2. Custom design or a starting template

A custom design is built around your brand and your customers. A template is faster to start but harder to make your own. Many good projects sit in between: a custom design system applied consistently across the product.

3. What the product has to do

  • Accounts and sign-in
  • Payments and orders
  • Search and filters
  • Booking and calendars
  • An admin panel so your team can manage content
  • Connections to other tools, such as payment gateways, maps, CRMs or delivery partners

Each feature has design, development and testing time behind it. Integrations with outside services often take longer than they look, because you depend on how those services work.

4. Platforms

A website runs everywhere. A mobile app may need to work on both iOS and Android, either from one shared codebase or built natively for each. Supporting more devices, screen sizes and older phones adds testing time.

5. Content

Who writes the words and supplies the photos? Content that is ready on day one keeps a project moving. Content that has to be created, translated or photographed is real work and should be in the plan.

6. What happens after launch

Hosting, security updates, store submissions and ongoing improvements are part of owning a website or app. A good quote is clear about what is included at launch and what care looks like afterwards.

How to compare quotes fairly

  1. Ask each studio to list the screens and features they have priced.
  2. Check who owns the code, the designs and the accounts at the end.
  3. Ask what is included after launch.
  4. Look at real work they have shipped, not only mockups.
