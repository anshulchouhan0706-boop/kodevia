How to publish your app on Google Play and the App Store

A step-by-step overview of publishing a mobile app on Google Play and Apple's App Store: accounts, listings, reviews and common rejection reasons.

Building the app is only half the journey. To reach customers it has to pass review and be listed on Google Play and Apple's App Store. Here is what the process involves, so launch day has no surprises.

1. Developer accounts in your name

Each store requires a developer account: the Google Play Console and the Apple Developer Program. Apple charges a yearly fee and Google a one-time registration fee; check the current amounts on each site. Open the accounts in your company's name so you always own your app and its listing. Organisation accounts may need business verification, so start early.

2. Prepare the store listing

  • App name, short and full description
  • Icon and screenshots for the required device sizes
  • Category, contact email and support website
  • A privacy policy page on your website
  • Data safety and privacy answers describing what data the app collects

3. Test before you submit

Use TestFlight on iOS and testing tracks on Google Play to share the app with real testers first. Depending on the type of account, Google may require a closed test with a minimum number of testers before you can publish to everyone.

4. Submit for review

Both stores review apps before they go live, and review times vary. Apple's review in particular checks the app against detailed guidelines.

Common reasons apps are rejected

  • Crashes or broken features during review
  • Missing or vague privacy information
  • A login with no test account provided for the reviewer
  • Screenshots that do not match the app
  • An app that is only a website in a wrapper with no app-like value

5. After launch

Each year brings new iOS and Android versions and updated store policies. Apps need regular testing and updates to keep working and stay listed.
