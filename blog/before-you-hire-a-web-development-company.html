What to prepare before you hire a web development company

A practical checklist of what to have ready before you contact a web or app development company, so the project starts fast and stays on track.

The best projects start with a little preparation. You do not need a technical document. You need clear answers to a few simple questions, and a handful of materials gathered in one place.

1. Your goal, in one sentence

What should the website or app achieve? More enquiries, online sales, bookings, fewer phone calls, a better experience for existing customers? One clear goal helps every design decision later.

2. Who it is for

Describe your customers in plain words: who they are, what device they use, and what they are trying to get done when they arrive. If you have more than one type of customer, list them and say which matters most.

3. The must-haves and the nice-to-haves

Make two lists. Must-haves are the features without which the product fails its goal. Nice-to-haves can wait for a later phase. This is the single most useful thing you can bring, because it keeps the first version focused.

4. Examples you like, and why

Collect a few websites or apps you like, from any industry. For each one, write one line about what you like: the layout, the checkout, the photography, the tone. The reason matters more than the link.

5. Your brand materials

  • Logo files, ideally in vector format
  • Brand colours and fonts, if you have a guide
  • Photos of your products, team or space
  • Any existing copy you want to keep

6. Access and accounts

If you already have a domain, hosting, a current website, social profiles or app store accounts, note who controls them. Sorting out access early saves days at launch.

7. A decision maker and a rough timeline

Decide who will give feedback and sign off. Projects slow down most when feedback comes from many people at different times. Share any fixed dates, such as a product launch or an event, at the start.

Questions to ask the company

  • Can I see real projects you have shipped?
  • Who will I talk to during the project?
  • How will I see progress before launch?
  • Who owns the code and designs at the end?
  • What does support look like after launch?
